Gingham, a family wall display

Bring your own screen.

Put Gingham on an Android photo frame or an old tablet and it becomes the centerpiece of the kitchen: calendar, lists, weather and photos. The app is its own server. No hub, no account, no subscription.

Whatever screen you have

Ours is a 15.6-inch photo frame that was $50 on Amazon. An old tablet in a drawer, an eBay or Goodwill find: any Android touchscreen from 8.0 on will do.

The screen is the server

The server runs inside the app, on the screen itself. Nothing else in the house has to be on. Calendar links and tokens stay in the app’s own private storage.

No Gingham cloud

Nothing is tracked. Besides your own calendars and lists, a frame only asks the internet for the weather and, once a day, whether a newer Gingham exists. You can turn that check off in Settings. When the internet is out, the wall keeps the last good screen and says from when.

Lists, at the wall or from a phone.

Kept by Gingham, or in Todoist, Google Tasks, Microsoft To Do, a CalDAV server or Home Assistant. Check things off with a moment to undo.

What it runs on.

Any Android
8.0 or later, on a photo frame or a tablet. Built and tested on a 15.6-inch, 1920 × 1080 photo frame with Android 8.1 and 1 GB of memory.
The install
An APK from the latest release, not an app store. Most frames want gingham-32bit.apk (22 MB); gingham-either.apk (45 MB) runs on any Android.
Check first
A few photo frames lock their launcher so nothing else can be installed. Look for one that lets you install an APK, or use a tablet.

Plain Node. No packages. No build step.

One small program, on the frame or in a container.

Open source, and early.
